## Red team

- [data_conflict, high, rating too high, supported] The desk starts growth at 14.37% (growth_used), but the 10-K shows fiscal 2025 net revenue growth of 11% (PERF-2), with the largest segment, U.S. net revenue, up only 6% (PERF-3). The trailing figure is inflated by a mix shift toward faster-growing international (PERF-4). Starting growth should be 11%. (cites PERF-2, PERF-3, PERF-4)
- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] The model holds 14.37% growth for five years, but the year5_growth_cap of 10% only bites above 10% — so a 14.37% start decays to 10% by year 5, still far above the 6.12% implied_growth the market prices. The fade is too slow for a company whose U.S. revenue grows 6%. (cites implied_growth, year5_growth_cap, PERF-3)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] The rating ignores the moat evidence: ROIC of 45.9% against a 7.91% WACC, sales_to_capital of 5.0, cash_conversion of 1.03 and a 61.1% operating margin. Terminal excess return is set to 1.0 (full moat), yet the base fair value of 498 already embeds a fade to 6.12% implied growth — the market is pricing a moat far narrower than the fundamentals show. (cites roic, sales_to_capital, cash_conversion, operating_margin, terminal_excess_return)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] The desk's own bear case (222.58) assumes growth -4 points and margin -10% relative, but the 10-K shows every revenue line growing: service +9% (PERF-5), data processing +13% (PERF-6), international transactions +12% (PERF-7), international +15% (PERF-4).
