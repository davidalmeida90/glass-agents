## Red team

- [data_conflict, medium, rating too high, supported] The desk's growth_used of -0.21% comes from a period distorted by COVID-product collapse. The 10-K shows the underlying franchise grew: excluding Comirnaty and Paxlovid, revenues rose 6% operationally (PERF-3). Using the ex-COVID trend as the starting growth rate would raise fair value and argue against Underweight. (cites PERF-3, PERF-2)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] The desk's operating_margin of 24.69% is far below the Biopharma segment's economics: segment earnings of $29,342m on $61,199m of revenue imply a ~48% segment margin (PERF-7, PERF-6). If the DCF understates the core margin, base fair value is too low and the Underweight is too harsh. (cites PERF-7, PERF-6)
- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] The terminal value assumes 50.6% of today's excess return survives (terminal_share), but the desk's own roic of 7.92% is barely above wacc of 8.09% — the spread is negative. A negative current spread makes any positive terminal excess return unsupported; setting terminal_share to 0 would cut fair value and support a harsher rating. (cites roic, wacc, terminal_share, terminal_excess_return)
- [method, low, rating too low, supported] The DCF charges reinvestment against a sales_to_capital of 0.795 while capitalizing R&D and treating leases as debt; with a 0% effective tax rate (effective_tax) and a 4.75% terminal growth equal to the risk-free rate, the terminal assumptions are internally generous, so the bear case of 14.25 may be too punitive relative to the base. (cites sales_to_capital, effective_tax, terminal_growth, fair_value)
