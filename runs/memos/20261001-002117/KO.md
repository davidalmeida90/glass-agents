## Red team

- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] The moat test is applied to a 5-year ROIC history that the evidence does not supply; terminal_share 0.745 and terminal_excess_return 1.0 assume a wide moat persists forever. If the moat share were 0 (Damodaran's default), base fair value would fall well below 65.09, making the Sell even stronger. (cites terminal_share, terminal_excess_return, roic, wacc)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] The desk ignores the quality of the franchise: gross margin rose to 61.6% (PERF-3), operating margin 29.3%, cash conversion 1.216, and ROIC 17.5% versus a 7.6% WACC. A 1.32x price/fair-value on a business earning a 10-point excess return is a thin margin of safety for a Sell. (cites PERF-3, cash_conversion, roic, operating_margin, wacc)
- [data_conflict, medium, rating too low, supported] growth_used 5.11% is the trailing-twelve-month figure, but the 10-K evidence shows operating cash flow up 9% (PERF-10) and net income of $13.1bn (PERF-8). If the starting growth were nearer the 10-K's momentum, the base value would rise and the Sell would not hold. (cites PERF-10, PERF-8, growth_used, implied_growth)
- [method, low, rating too high, supported] implied_growth 9.99% is roughly double growth_used 5.11%, so the market price already embeds an acceleration the DCF does not grant. That gap is the desk's core Sell case, but it rests on a single noisy trailing-twelve-month growth input (growth_bounds clip), not on a durable trend. (cites implied_growth, growth_used, growth_bounds)
