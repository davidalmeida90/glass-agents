## Red team

- [data_conflict, high, rating too high, supported] The desk's growth_used of 5.11% is stale. The latest 10-Q shows revenue up 9% for the six months to July 3, 2026 (PERF-3), and operating cash flow swung from -$1,391m to +$7,543m (PERF-7). Using 5.11% understates the current trajectory, so the base fair value and the Sell rating are built on an outdated growth input. (cites PERF-3, PERF-7)
- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] The moat assumption is generous. terminal_share of 0.745 and terminal_excess_return of 1.0 assume the full ROIC-WACC spread (17.54% vs 7.6%) survives forever, yet the fade already caps growth at the risk-free rate. A wide-moat perpetuity on a 0.93 sales-to-capital staple is the single biggest lever in the base value. (cites terminal_share, terminal_excess_return, roic, sales_to_capital)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] Regulatory and health headwinds are ignored. RISK-1 and RISK-2 flag childhood chronic disease scrutiny and sweetened-beverage taxes/regulation; RISK-5 shows one bottler is 10% of revenue. These are real threats to the terminal value that the Sell rating does not need to lean on, but they also mean the bull case (116.73) is optimistic. (cites RISK-1, RISK-2, RISK-5)
- [method, medium, rating too low, supported] The WACC build uses a bottom-up beta of 0.8 clipped at the 0.8 floor, while the regression beta is 0.317. For a staple with 0.575 unlevered industry beta, the floor forces an artificially high cost of equity, depressing fair value and inflating the Sell signal. (cites beta_bounds, wacc)
