## Red team

- [method, high, rating too high, supported] The DCF holds 27.65% growth for five years, but the 10-K shows 22% growth (PERF-2) and the six-month 10-Q comparison implies ~30% off a small base (PERF-6). A 27.65% start is above the company's own fiscal-year rate; the base case leans on the most optimistic reading of one noisy trailing window. (cites PERF-2, PERF-6, growth_used, growth_bounds)
- [method, high, rating too low, supported] The model decays growth to 10% by year 5 and then to 4.75%, yet the desk's own implied_growth is only 3.31% — below the terminal rate. The market price of 725 implies far less than the base fair value of 1407; the rating may be too low if the fade is too harsh on a business compounding revenue at 22-30%. (cites implied_growth, terminal_growth, PERF-2, PERF-6)
- [business, medium, rating too high, supported] Reality Labs lost $19.2bn in 2025 (PERF-10) and capex was $69.7bn (CAP-7), with acquisitions jumping to $4.2bn (CAP-8). The model's sales_to_capital of 0.617 and 23.4% ROIC assume reinvestment earns the current spread; heavy AI/RL spending may not, so terminal_share of 0.786 is generous. (cites PERF-10, CAP-7, CAP-8, sales_to_capital, terminal_share)
- [method, medium, rating too low, supported] Operating margin is R&D-adjusted to 52.3% from 38.1% reported (operating_margin_reported), and SBC of 11% of revenue is expensed (sbc_to_revenue). Both depress owner earnings versus cash conversion of 1.403 (PERF-5, PERF-7); the base value may understate cash generation. (cites operating_margin, operating_margin_reported, sbc_to_revenue, cash_conversion, PERF-5, PERF-7)
