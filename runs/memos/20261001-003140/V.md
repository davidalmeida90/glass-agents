## Red team

- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] The moat assumption is the whole ballgame: terminal_share 0.742 keeps 74% of a 38-point ROIC-WACC spread forever, and terminal_excess_return 1.0 rests on a 5-year ROIC history that is largely a litigation-tainted, incentive-heavy accounting artifact. Damodaran's default is zero excess return; a 0.5 narrow-moat share would cut base fair value materially. (cites terminal_share, terminal_excess_return, roic, moat_margin)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] The rating ignores the durability of the core: 61% operating margin, 5.0x sales-to-capital, 1.03 cash conversion, and international revenue up 15% (PERF-4) versus U.S. up only 6% (PERF-3). A 14.4% trailing growth rate is being faded to 10% by year 5 and 4.75% terminal, understating the runway in cross-border and value-added services. (cites PERF-4, PERF-3, operating_margin, sales_to_capital, cash_conversion, growth_used)
- [business, medium, rating too high, supported] Concentration and regulatory risk are underweighted: one client is 11% of net revenue (PERF-10), the U.S. is 39% (PERF-9), Reg II was vacated (RISK-5), New Zealand adopted cross-border interchange caps and Australia proposed them (RISK-6), and stablecoins could disintermediate cross-border and B2B (RISK-8). Any of these compresses the 61% margin the DCF holds flat. (cites PERF-10, PERF-9, RISK-5, RISK-6, RISK-8)
- [method, low, rating too low, supported] The method penalizes a low-beta, net-cash-like compounder: beta is clipped to the 0.8 floor (regression 0.848, industry unlevered 0.328) and mature_beta keeps it there, while the 4.75% terminal growth cap equals the risk-free rate. Both fixed choices mechanically depress fair value for a AAA-rated (coverage 42x) franchise. (cites beta_bounds, mature_beta, terminal_growth_cap, rating_spreads)
