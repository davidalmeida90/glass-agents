## Red team

- [data_conflict, high, rating too high, supported] The desk's growth_used of 14.24% is a nine-month stub (PERF-6/PERF-7: $364,357m vs $313,695m = 16.2% for nine months), not a full trailing-twelve-month figure, and it is far above the 10-K's 6% (PERF-2). The DCF holds 14.24% for five years, so the base value is built on a rate the audited annual evidence contradicts. (cites PERF-2, PERF-6, PERF-7, growth_used)
- [business, high, rating too low, supported] The rating ignores the balance sheet and capital return: $156.7bn cash and securities (CAP-1) against $90.7bn term debt (CAP-3) plus $8.0bn commercial paper (CAP-5) is roughly $58bn net cash, while buybacks of $89.3bn (CAP-6) and dividends of $15.4bn (CAP-8) are funded from $111.5bn of operating cash flow (PERF-5). (cites CAP-1, CAP-3, CAP-5, CAP-6, CAP-8, PERF-5)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] The desk's own figures show a business earning far above its cost of capital: ROIC 58.6% versus WACC 10.2%, sales-to-capital 2.21, cash conversion 1.03 and a 36.9% R&D-adjusted operating margin. A 1.11x price/fair-value Underweight treats a wide-moat compounder as if its excess returns were about to vanish. (cites roic, wacc, sales_to_capital, cash_conversion, operating_margin)
- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] The method's fixed assumptions flatter the value: terminal_share 0.708 puts most of the value beyond year 10, terminal_excess_return 1.0 assumes the entire 48-point ROIC-WACC spread survives forever, and the 10.19% WACC uses a bottom-up beta of 1.344 while the regression beta is 1.047. Each choice raises fair value and pushes the rating toward Underweight. (cites terminal_share, terminal_excess_return, wacc, beta_method)
