## Red team

- [data_conflict, medium, rating too high, supported] The desk's growth_used of 6.1% is below the 10-Q's own six-month net sales growth of 6.5% (PERF-5), and the 10-K shows 4.7% (PERF-2). Using the slower 10-K-era figure understates the current trajectory, so the Sell is built on a stale growth input. (cites PERF-5, PERF-2, growth_used)
- [method, high, rating too low, supported] The DCF holds growth at 6.1% for five years then fades to 4.75%, but implied_growth of 17.17% is what the market price requires. The gap is so wide that the Sell rests entirely on the fade assumption, not on any evidence about Walmart's business. (cites implied_growth, growth_used, terminal_growth)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] Cash conversion of 1.733 (OCF $41.6bn vs net income $21.9bn, PERF-4/ACC-1) and $8.1bn of buybacks plus $7.5bn of dividends (CAP-5, CAP-6) show the business funds large shareholder returns from operations. A Sell rating ignores this cash-generative reality. (cites PERF-4, ACC-1, CAP-5, CAP-6, cash_conversion)
- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] Terminal share of 75.8% of value with terminal_roic held at 16.13% and a wide-moat share of 1.0 assumes today's excess return persists forever. For a low-margin retailer (operating_margin 4.43%) facing AI-driven competition (RISK-6), that is an aggressive terminal assumption that inflates fair value. (cites terminal_share, terminal_roic, operating_margin, RISK-6)
