## Red team

- [method, high, rating too high, supported] The model routes insurers to the excess-return model, yet the desk's stated method is justified P/B with ROE -/+2pt bear/bull. The rating rests on a base fair value of 413.97 (1.25x price) while the industry-multiples cross-check gives 282.39 — 0.68x price, below the 0.80 Buy line. The second method contradicts the Overweight. (cites multiples_check, fair_value, model_routes, price)
- [data_conflict, high, rating too high, supported] The model uses roe 0.1373, but the industry ROE is 0.1871 and the multiples check values CB at 282.39 using that industry ROE. If CB's own ROE is below the industry's, the ROE-adjusted P/B cross-check (book 190.24 x 2.02 x 0.1373/0.1871) implies roughly 282, not 414. The base fair value looks inflated relative to the evidence. (cites roe, multiples_check, book_value_per_share, implied_roe)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] Operating cash flow was $12,816m in 2025 against net income of $10,622m — cash conversion above 1.2x, and the first half of 2026 ran $7,677m versus $5,117m a year earlier. The rating's excess-return model on book value ignores this cash generation and the $18.1bn investment gain, understating earning power. (cites PERF-3, PERF-2, PERF-10, PERF-9)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] Life Insurance segment income rose 8.8% and life deposits grew $197m in the recent quarter, while policy benefits rose to $5,460m on international life growth. The model's normalized 5-year average ROE and 0.5 financial moat cap may understate a business with visible premium and deposit momentum. (cites PERF-8, PERF-7, PERF-6, normalized_financial_roe, financial_moat_cap)
