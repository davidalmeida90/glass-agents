## Red team

- [method, high, rating too low, supported] The DDM caps stage-1 dividend growth at 8% (ddm growth_bounds), yet growth_used is already 0.08 and the model's own growth_blend allows up to 30%. A utility with a $24.6bn capex program (CAP-7) and rising share count (CAP-8) is being valued on a payout-constrained growth ceiling that binds immediately, understating fair value. (cites growth_used, ddm, CAP-7, CAP-8)
- [method, medium, rating too high, supported] The DDM grows dividends at revenue growth, but dividends are paid out of earnings, not revenue. Net income fell to $6.84bn in 2025 from $6.95bn (ACC-1) while dividends paid were $4,680m (CAP-6) — a payout near 68% with capex of $24,606m (CAP-7) funded by $23,394m of new debt (CAP-3). Growth funded by leverage, not by the dividend stream, is not captured. (cites ACC-1, CAP-6, CAP-7, CAP-3)
- [data_conflict, medium, rating too high, supported] growth_used of 0.08 is the top of the DDM's own bound and sits above the 10-K fiscal-year trajectory: total revenues fell from $28,114m in 2023 (PERF-5) to $24,753m in 2024 (PERF-2). A 3-year CAGR on those figures is negative, so the blend's 50% cagr_3y weight should pull starting growth well below 8%. (cites PERF-5, PERF-2, growth_used, growth_blend)
- [business, medium, rating too low, supported] The rating ignores the earnings mix: NEER contributed $1,634m of the $3,144m quarterly net income (PERF-10) and $2,299m in 2024 (PERF-3) — unregulated, higher-multiple growth (Duane Arnold restart, RISK-6) that a regulated-utility dividend discount model cannot price. Fair value is likely understated. (cites PERF-10, PERF-3, RISK-6)
