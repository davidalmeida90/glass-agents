## Red team

- [method, high, rating too high, supported] The DCF's own implied_growth of 73% is unreachable: the model must assume growth far above the 13.75% used and above the 18% six-month rate (PERF-9) to justify the price. A 2.17x price/base gap means the Sell rests on a DCF whose inputs cannot be reconciled with the company's actual trajectory. (cites implied_growth, PERF-9, growth_used)
- [business, high, rating too low, supported] AWS grew 20% to $128.7bn (PERF-4) and International 13% (PERF-5), with consolidated sales up 18% in the latest six months (PERF-9) — accelerating, not decelerating. Holding growth at 13.75% then fading to 4.75% understates a mix shifting toward the highest-margin, highest-growth segment. (cites PERF-4, PERF-5, PERF-9, growth_used)
- [data_conflict, medium, rating too low, supported] The desk's 12.08% operating margin ignores segment mix: North America alone earned $29.6bn on $426.3bn (PERF-6, PERF-3) while International ran $157.1bn of expenses on $161.9bn of sales (PERF-7), i.e. ~3% margin. AWS's 20% growth at far higher margins should lift consolidated margin above the 12.08% used. (cites PERF-6, PERF-3, PERF-7, operating_margin)
- [method, medium, rating too low, supported] The multiples cross-check is discarded: industry EV/EBIT of 32.4 gives $272/share versus the $246.67 price, yet the DCF/multiples ratio of 0.42 is treated as confirming a Sell. The second method says the stock is roughly fairly valued, not 2.17x overvalued. (cites multiples_check, ev_to_ebit, market_ev_to_ebit)
